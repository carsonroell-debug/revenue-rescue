# Revenue Rescue — Terms of Service (Draft)

**Status:** pre-launch draft. This document should receive appropriate legal
review and final business details before public publication.

## 1. Service

Revenue Rescue provides automated analysis of public websites for potential
commerce and monetization problems and may monitor those websites over time.

Reports and alerts are informational tools, not guarantees of revenue,
availability, attribution, or business outcomes.

## 2. Acceptable use

Users must use Revenue Rescue lawfully and responsibly.

Do not use the service to:

- probe private networks or non-public systems;
- submit credentials or private URLs as scan targets;
- overload, disrupt, or attack third-party services;
- evade access controls;
- use results for unlawful activity.

Users are responsible for having any permission required for their intended use
of a target website.

## 3. Automated access

Revenue Rescue uses bounded automated requests to public websites. Usage may be
limited, throttled, suspended, or blocked to protect Revenue Rescue and target
sites.

## 4. Findings and uncertainty

Revenue Rescue follows a precision-first design, but automated analysis can
still be incomplete or wrong.

Some results are intentionally treated as inconclusive, including many
bot-blocks, timeouts, and transient network failures.

Users should verify material findings before making business or financial
decisions.

## 5. Monitoring

A monitor compares current public-site evidence with a prior baseline.
Websites can change for many legitimate reasons, and a detected change is not
automatically proof of lost revenue.

## 6. Availability

The service may be modified, rate-limited, interrupted, or discontinued.
Specific service-level commitments should only be stated in a separate written
agreement.

## 7. Fees

Pricing and paid-plan terms must be finalized before public launch. This draft
does not commit Revenue Rescue to a free or paid business model.

## 8. Intellectual property

Revenue Rescue's software, branding, and proprietary analysis logic remain the
property of their respective owner(s). Users retain rights in data they submit
to the extent they possess those rights.

## 9. Warranty and liability

The final public terms should include jurisdiction-appropriate warranty
disclaimers and limitation-of-liability language after legal review.

## 10. Changes and contact

The final public terms should include an effective date, governing business
identity/contact details, and a process for notifying users of material
changes.
